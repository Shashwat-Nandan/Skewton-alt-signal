# Hidden Markov Models for Market Regime Detection

> Source: an external strategy write-up supplied to this repo (no book/paper
> citation — author/venue unknown), summarizing a Gaussian-HMM approach to
> regime detection and regime-adaptive position sizing/factor rotation. This
> doc extracts the reusable concepts, **stress-tests the claims against our
> own codebase and constraints**, and lays out a concrete, scoped path if we
> decide to build it. It is a study/reference note, not a recommendation to
> ship the code as-is — the validation numbers in the source (Sharpe 1.22,
> -19.5% max drawdown on SPY 2004-2025) are the *author's* backtest, not ours,
> and per Rule 12 (Fail Loud) should not be repeated as if we'd verified them.

## The core idea

Returns aren't generated by one stationary process. Volatility clustering,
momentum, and mean-reversion all show up in the same asset at different
times because the asset is switching between latent **regimes** — bull,
bear, neutral — that aren't directly observable but leave fingerprints in
the distribution of returns and volatility.

An HMM formalizes this as three objects:
- **Hidden state** `z_t ∈ {0, 1, 2}` — the regime at time `t`.
- **Transition matrix** `A_ij = P(z_t = j | z_{t-1} = i)` — regime
  persistence/switching probabilities.
- **Emission distribution** `r_t | z_t ~ N(μ_{z_t}, σ²_{z_t})` — each regime
  has its own return mean/variance (and in the multivariate case, its own
  mean/covariance over a feature vector like `[return, realized_vol]`).

Baum-Welch (EM) fits `A` and the per-regime distributions from history;
Viterbi (or `predict_proba` for the soft/posterior version) decodes the
state sequence. None of this predicts the future — it labels the present,
with a lag, conditioned on data already observed.

## What the source material actually proposes

1. **`MarketRegimeHMM`** — a 3-state `hmmlearn.GaussianHMM` fit on two
   features (daily return, 20-day realized vol), with regimes labeled
   BULL/NEUTRAL/BEAR by sorting on mean return. `predict_proba` gives a
   soft regime distribution rather than a hard label.
2. **`RegimeAdaptiveStrategy`** — blends a momentum sub-signal and a
   mean-reversion sub-signal, weighted by `P(BULL)` / `P(NEUTRAL)`
   respectively, with `P(BEAR)` driving a defensive scaling-down of total
   exposure. Soft (probability-weighted) switching instead of hard
   regime-flip switching, explicitly to cut turnover.
3. **`RegimeFactorRotator`** — a cross-sectional factor-rotation overlay
   (momentum/quality/value/low-vol/size weights blended by regime
   probability). This assumes a multi-asset factor portfolio — see
   "What doesn't map" below.
4. **Failure modes called out**: lookahead bias (must walk-forward fit,
   never fit on the full sample then backtest), regime instability
   (>3-4 states stop having economic meaning and flip erratically — cap at
   2-3), transition lag (Viterbi/posterior regime confirmation lags the
   true switch by ~5-15 trading days — this is the cost of the approach,
   not a bug), and non-stationarity (retrain on a rolling window, e.g.
   monthly on a 2-year lookback — a regime model fit on 2010-2015 will not
   recognize 2020).
5. **Production checklist**: monthly retrain, stability monitor (kill
   switch: >3 regime flips in 10 days → cut position sizing), report
   posterior probabilities, not point labels.

The mechanics above are standard and the failure-mode list is honest and
worth keeping regardless of whether we adopt the specific code. The
validation table in the source (Sharpe 0.55 → 1.22, max DD -55% → -19.5% on
SPY) is asserted, not something we ran — treat it as "this is the kind of
improvement HMM regime-gating can plausibly produce," not as a number this
repo would reproduce on NIFTY options or NSE equities.

## What doesn't map to this repo

- **Cross-sectional factor rotation** (`RegimeFactorRotator`) assumes a
  multi-asset factor portfolio (momentum/value/quality/size/low-vol
  buckets across many names) we don't run. `varsity_equity_swing` scores
  single Nifty-200 names on trend + ATR + optional Market Profile/OI/FII-DII
  overlays, not a factor portfolio; `pair_trading` is two-leg cointegration.
  There's no place in this codebase a 5-factor weight vector plugs into
  today. Skip this piece unless we build a cross-sectional equity sleeve.
- **SPY-style single-index regime → momentum-vs-mean-reversion switch**:
  our strategies are not "pick momentum or mean-reversion for the same
  instrument." `taleb_karpathy` is a vol-harvesting straddle, `pair_trading`
  is permanently mean-reverting by construction (cointegration), and
  `varsity_equity_swing` is permanently trend-following by construction
  (Varsity Module 9 long-only swing). The source's "soft-switch between two
  sub-strategies" pattern has no natural target here — what *does* map is
  using a regime signal as an **exposure/sizing gate** on a strategy that
  already has a fixed thesis (see below).

## What we already have (don't rebuild it)

We already have two non-HMM "regime" mechanisms, and Rule 7 (surface
conflicts, don't average) means a third must be clearly distinct from both,
not a silent blend:

- **`core/regime_classifier.py`** — deterministic, rule-based classifier over
  four *option-surface* observables (IV percentile, RV/IV ratio, skew
  percentile, vol-of-vol) → one of six trade structures
  (`STRADDLE`/`CALENDAR_SHORT_FRONT`/`RISK_REVERSAL_LONG_PUT`/`BACKSPREAD`/
  `ASYMMETRIC_STRANGLE`/`NO_TRADE`). Wired into `taleb_karpathy.py:564-588`
  via `RegimeFeatures`/`classify()`. This is "regime" in the Taleb Ch.15/16
  sense (what distribution shape the option surface implies), entirely
  different from "is the market bull/bear/neutral."
- **`research/analyze_rv_iv_regime.py`** — an offline analysis script (not wired into
  any live strategy) that computes a daily RV/IV ratio and reports threshold
  admission rates and ratio-crossing dates. Descriptive, not predictive; no
  model is fit, no state is inferred — it's a transition-counting report
  used to sanity-check `regime_classifier`'s thresholds against bhavcopy
  history.

An HMM-based **macro/trend regime** (bull/bear/neutral on price+vol, the
thing the source material actually describes) would be a *third*, distinct
notion of regime — index-direction/vol-state, not vol-surface-shape. If
built, name it so it can't be confused with the existing one — e.g.
`hmm_regime.py` / `MacroRegimeHMM`, never `regime_classifier` v2 or anything
that overloads the existing `Structure`/`RegimeFeatures` names.

## Where it could plug in, concretely

The only place a bull/bear/neutral macro signal has an honest use in this
codebase today is as an **exposure multiplier** on a strategy whose
direction/thesis is already fixed:

- **`varsity_equity_swing`** (`strategies/varsity_equity_swing.py`,
  long-only trend swing on Nifty 200) is the best fit. It already computes
  ADX-based trend strength (`strategies/_indicators.py:49`) per name but has
  no market-wide regime gate. A `P(BEAR)`-driven size cut (scale new-entry
  size by `1 - P(BEAR)`, or pause new entries above a BEAR-confidence
  threshold) is a direct, low-risk application of the source's "soft
  defensive scaling" idea — without inventing a momentum/mean-reversion
  switch the strategy was never designed to need.
- **`pair_trading`** could use `P(BEAR)`/instability flag to cut overall
  book leverage in stressed regimes (correlations/spread behavior degrade
  in crises), but this is speculative — cointegration-based mean reversion
  doesn't have the same regime-dependence the source assumes for
  single-asset momentum-vs-reversion switching. Lower priority.
- **`taleb_karpathy`** already has its own (vol-surface) regime gate; a
  macro HMM regime is redundant for structure selection. The only arguably
  useful addition is using regime-instability (the >3-flips-in-10-days kill
  switch) as one more circuit-breaker input alongside the existing
  daily-loss breaker — also speculative, not a clear win.

## Implementation cost if we proceed

- **New dependency**: `hmmlearn` is not in `requirements.in`/`.lock`. The
  source's multivariate Gaussian emission (return + realized-vol jointly,
  with a full covariance per regime) is `hmmlearn.GaussianHMM`'s actual
  strength; `statsmodels` (already a dependency) ships
  `tsa.regime_switching.markov_regression.MarkovRegression`, which is a
  legitimate Markov-switching/EM model but is built for a single endogenous
  series with regime-dependent mean/variance, not a clean multivariate
  feature vector. Don't reach for it just to dodge a new dependency — that
  would be the "average two patterns" failure (Rule 7) in dependency form,
  not feature parity. If we build this, add `hmmlearn` honestly.
- **Walk-forward discipline is the whole game.** The source's own failure
  catalogue (lookahead bias is failure mode #1) is exactly our existing
  autoresearch hygiene (`docs/research/autoresearch_pattern.md` — windowed
  splits, hold-out validation) applied to a new model class. A `hmm_regime.py`
  module would need the same train-only-on-preceding-window discipline our
  sweep scripts already enforce; reuse that pattern rather than re-deriving
  it.
- **Backtest harness**: would follow the existing
  `backtest_*.py` convention (a new `backtest_regime_overlay.py` comparing
  with/without the regime gate on `varsity_equity_swing`'s existing
  signals) rather than a bespone harness — match Rule 11 (codebase
  conventions) on argument parsing, output format, and where results land
  in `data_cache/`.
- **Retrain cadence**: the source recommends monthly; we already have a
  weekly cadence pattern (`taleb-autoresearch.timer`, Sat 10:00 IST) for a
  different model. A monthly HMM retrain would be a new, slower-frequency
  cron, not a reuse of the existing one — don't force it onto the weekly
  timer just because one exists.

## Recommendation

Don't build the full source (3-strategy soft switch + factor rotation) —
most of it doesn't have a target in this codebase. If there's appetite for
this, the scoped version worth prototyping is:

1. `hmm_regime.py` — 3-state `GaussianHMM` on NIFTY (or the relevant
   index) returns + 20-day realized vol, walk-forward fit, exposing
   `regime_probabilities()` and the stability check (>3 flips/10 days).
2. A backtest comparing `varsity_equity_swing` with vs. without a
   `1 - P(BEAR)` position-size multiplier, over the same data the strategy's
   existing backtest (`research/backtest_varsity_equity.py`) already uses.
3. Only if step 2 shows a real, walk-forward (not in-sample) improvement,
   wire it into the live strategy behind a feature flag, with the
   instability kill switch from day one.

Steps 1-2 are a few hundred lines and one new pinned dependency — small
enough to timebox and throw away if the backtest doesn't show an edge.
Skip the factor-rotation class and the momentum/mean-reversion soft-switch
class entirely; neither has a home here.
